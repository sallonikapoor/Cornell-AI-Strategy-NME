HOOK

Work backward

When a client asks for something specific, they're usually guessing at a fix for a problem they feel but haven't pinned down. That's normal.

Notice that the real problem points to different solutions than the request did. If leadership doesn't trust the numbers, a prettier dashboard won't help. If report-writing time goes into collecting data, a better writing tool only fixes half of it.

LIVE INTERVIEW

What unlocked it?

Where do you keep track of each repair’s status?

Copperline's mechanics get interrupted dozens of times a week by repair-status questions because status lives on a whiteboard no one else can see, which slows down repairs and frustrates customers.

LIVE INTERVIEW

Phase 1: Get the data in order.

Track repair status digitally (Square or a simple shared spreadsheet) instead of on the whiteboard. Code sends customers an automatic text or email when their bike's status changes.

LIVE INTERVIEW

Phase 2: Help with the other emails.

An LLM drafts replies to questions about hours and general info, and staff review them before they're sent. It never quotes prices; it links to the posted price list instead.

LIVE INTERVIEW

Out of scope for now

A public chatbot.

LIVE INTERVIEW

What done looks like

Cut 'is it ready' emails by half within a month, measured by tagging emails in the inbox for two weeks before and after. Guardrail: zero wrong price quotes.

LIVE INTERVIEW

The most valuable first step

Notice that the most valuable first step isn't AI at all. That happens a lot. Sometimes the best scope fixes how information is tracked, and the AI comes second.

METRICS

Measure the right thing

Time to resolve means the elapsed time a customer waits, including time in a queue. Staff time means the minutes someone actively spends on the request. Choose the measure that matches the problem you want to solve.

Don't know the baseline yet? That's fine. Measuring it becomes the first task. You can't show improvement without a starting point.

METRICS

What could go wrong?

Answer every “is it ready?” email instantly with “yes.” Response time improves; customers arrive for unfinished repairs. Check every status against the repair record.

Stop counting repeat emails. The reported volume falls, but customers are still waiting. Count every incoming message using the same rule before and after.

Mark repairs complete early to trigger notifications. The dashboard looks faster; the work is unfinished. Only a mechanic can confirm completion.

METRICS

Keep the guardrail

The guardrail is the one people forget, and it ties back to Week 1. Speeding up a process with AI is easy if you stop checking the work. The guardrail makes sure you didn't.

        SCOPE

        Assumptions and risks

        Assumptions: The ticketing system can export order data. Staff will review drafted replies within a day.

        Risks: Some emails won't include enough information to find the order. Special-circumstance requests may be more common than expected.

        Every scope rests on things you’re assuming are true. Write them down so everyone knows why a plan changed.

        SCOPE

        Five common mistakes

        1. Solving the request, not the problem. Building the chatbot the owner asked for.
        2. Vague success. “Faster” or “better,” with no number attached.
        3. Too big a first version. Trying to automate the whole process at once.
        4. Assuming the data exists. Discovering mid-project that it’s on a whiteboard.
        5. No guardrail. Hitting the speed target while errors quietly go up.

        ACTIVITY / SCOPE

        Spot the mistake: Moss Laundry

        The owner requested a dashboard. The team commits to one before asking what is going wrong. They have checked the data, bounded the work, and defined a measurable target and error guardrail.

        Solving the request, not the problem. The team accepted the requested solution without finding the underlying problem.

        00:00

        ACTIVITY / SCOPE

        Spot the mistake: Juniper Gym

        Discovery identifies slow cancellation replies. A small email pilot uses verified membership records and staff approval to prevent incorrect cancellations. Its only success goal is “make replies better.”

        Vague success. There is no measurable baseline and target.

        00:00

        ACTIVITY / SCOPE

        Spot the mistake: Pebble Florist

        Discovery finds time lost retyping orders. The pilot has a clear time target, staff approval, and a narrow scope. The plan depends on a digital order log nobody has checked exists.

        Assuming the data exists. Check where the order data lives before committing to the plan.

        00:00

        ACTIVITY / SCOPE

        Spot the mistake: Harbor Tutors

        Discovery finds slow scheduling replies. A narrow pilot uses verified calendars and aims to reduce median reply time from two days to four hours in a month, measured from timestamps. There is no check for double bookings.

        No guardrail. Add a check that prevents a faster reply from creating a double booking.

        Team quiz 3

        Why bother writing down what's out of scope?

        It protects your team's time and sets the client's expectations. When a new request comes up mid-project, you can point to what everyone already agreed on.

        ACTIVITY / WRAP

        Team quiz 4

        Discovery shows the data you'd need doesn't exist yet. Now what?

        Make getting the data part of the scope, usually the first part. An AI tool can't use information that isn't recorded anywhere, like Copperline's whiteboard.
